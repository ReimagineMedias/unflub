You flubbed. We unflubbed.

I type fast, and I make a lot of typos. On my phone they fix themselves. On my laptop, in the places I type
most, they don't. AI prompt boxes, chat apps and web forms mostly leave them sitting there.

So I built unflub: phone-style autocorrect for every text field on Windows. You finish a word, and if it was
a typo, it's fixed before your next key lands. If it got it wrong, Backspace puts your word back, and it
remembers.

A few things I cared about:

It's light. About 0.1 ms to decide on a word, about 20 MB of memory, and nothing while you're not typing.

It's private. Nothing you type leaves your computer. It checks for updates once a day without an ID, and
anonymous usage counts and crash reports are on unless you turn them off on first run.

It stays out of the way. Code, passwords and terminals are left alone, and every setting is visible. There
are no hidden ones.

It's free and open source under the MIT license, so anyone can read exactly what it does.

Windows first, English for now. macOS and Linux are next.

Download: https://unflub.rmgn.ca
Code: https://github.com/ReimagineMedias/unflub

If you try it, I'd like to hear which app it misbehaved in.
